When you're unsure about changing jobs: three things to separate first

"Should I stay, or should I go?" The same question loops in your head, late into the night, without landing anywhere. If that sounds familiar, you're in good company.

When the fog won't lift, it's rarely because you lack willpower or can't make decisions. More often, several separate questions have fused into a single knot. Before rushing to an answer, here are three things worth pulling apart.

1. Separate "what I dislike" from "what I can change"

Write down what's bothering you right now. Then ask of each item: could this actually change where I am? Is it the people, the work itself, or the way you work? The things that only a move would fix, and the things that might shift through a conversation or a transfer, are genuinely different problems.

2. Tell "moving away" apart from "moving toward"

Most reasons to change jobs mix the wish to get away from something with the wish to move toward something. Neither is wrong. But decisions made purely to escape tend to carry the same discomfort into the next place. It's worth looking honestly at which one is driving you today.

3. Try setting a tentative "by when"

A question with no deadline stays open forever. Try asking, "Where would I need to be three months from now to feel at peace with this?" It isn't a deadline to decide — it's a marker that helps your thinking move forward, and it makes the small next step easier to see.

Even sitting with these three on your own can thin the fog a little. When the loop keeps going anyway, talking it through out loud with someone is often the shortcut. A 45-minute session is built around exactly this kind of sorting.
